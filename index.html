The Web-Blower Case

Posted: Friday, July 31, 11:45 PM

The flashbulbs are getting blindingly bright these days. Or maybe my eyes are just getting more sensitive to the harsh light after working the graveyard shift for so long. Either way, the swarm of reporters camped out on the precinct steps tonight made me want to retreat back into the comforting, pitch-black shadows of the alleyway. I despise cameras. They capture things that shouldn't be captured, leave permanent records, and the sudden burst of light feels like a physical strike against my retinas. I slipped in through the loading dock to avoid them.

They were all out there hunting for a scoop on the Cassandra Harlough murder.

The press has taken to calling it the "Rumor Has It" case—a tacky, sensationalized moniker for a genuinely tragic end. I'm calling it "Web-Blower" instead. Cassandra was found in her study, and the scene was… messy. Most of the uniforms were holding their noses, but to me, the air in that room was rich, heavy, and metallic. It’s a scent that always makes the back of my throat burn with an old, familiar thirst, one I have to strictly manage with heavily iron-fortified "supplements" from my private thermos.

Cassandra was at the center of a very tangled web of local secrets, hence the media's nickname for the investigation. I spent hours analyzing the blood spatter—a subject I consider myself somewhat of an ancient connoisseur of. The trajectory didn't match the angle of the window where the detectives assumed the shooter stood. There was no reflection of a struggle in the antique silver-backed mirror on her vanity, though to be fair, I never like looking into silver mirrors myself anyway. They always make me feel strangely absent.

We brought in two suspects today, but their pulses were steady, their skin flushed with the steady, rhythmic heartbeat of innocent people. You can always hear it if you listen closely enough. The real killer was cold, calculated, and moved in the dark. Just like me. I'll catch them by Monday.

Weekend Addendum: Tools of the Trade

Posted: Saturday, August 1, 9:00 AM

As the sun is agonizingly bright outside today, I’ve drawn the blackout curtains, poured myself a thick, dark mug of something restorative, and sat down to tackle the mountains of incident reports the Harlough case generated.

Since it’s the weekend, it’s time for a stationery review. Filling out triplicate forms requires the right instrument, and lately, I’ve been heavily scrutinizing the structural differences between needle-tip liquid ink pens and porous point pens.

For the last century or so, I’ve appreciated a bold line, but the needle-tip liquid ink pen has been a revelation for my tiny, cramped margin notes. The tubular metal tip provides absolute precision. The liquid ink flows with a viscosity that is incredibly satisfying—smooth, dark, and permanent. However, it demands a light touch. If you press too hard, the sharp tip risks tearing through cheap precinct paper like a fang through soft tissue.

On the other hand, the porous point pen—often made of felt or nylon—offers a completely different tactile experience. It has a slight, satisfying drag on the paper, a quiet friction that I find grounding when the precinct gets too loud. It lays down a thicker, more assertive line, perfect for crossing out the absolute nonsense the day-shift detectives leave on my desk. The downside? The porous tips can fray over time if you write with a heavy hand, and the ink tends to feather on lower-quality legal pads.

The Verdict: I’m keeping the needle-tip liquid ink for my personal case ledger where precision matters, and the porous point for aggressively signing off on official department memos.
